# User Discovery Interview 1

> **Privacy Notice**: Real names, emails, and student IDs omitted per course guidelines.

- **Interviewee Role**: Sophomore Computer Science Major, Gonzaga University
- **Interviewer Role**: Student Systems Analyst
- **Date**: Fall 2026
- **Context**: Foley Library 2nd floor, during midterms study period

---

## Background & Current Habits
- Carries an average course load of 16 credits including two lab-heavy programming courses.
- Studies primarily on a laptop in 2- to 3-hour blocks between morning and evening classes.
- Currently uses a loose combination of Google Calendar for assignment deadlines and the built-in macOS Clock app for study sprints.

---

## Interview Notes & Observations

- **Previous attempts**: Tried using Toggl Track in September after seeing a YouTube study-productivity video. Used it for 5 consecutive days before stopping.
- **Pain points**:
  - "The biggest issue was running a timer for BMIS homework, getting called by a roommate to get coffee, and coming back 3 hours later to find a 4-hour session logged. Editing the start and stop times on desktop took too many clicks, so I just ignored the app the next day."
  - "When I'm already stressed about a deadline, filling out project tags, client fields, and billable status fields in Notion or Toggl feels like homework on top of homework."
- **Workarounds**: Now writes rough estimates in a paper planner at the end of the day, but admits those estimates are inaccurate by $\pm 40\%$.
- **Wants vs. Needs**: Wants to know how much time is actually spent writing code versus getting stuck debugging, but refuses to use any tool that requires more than two clicks to log a block.
